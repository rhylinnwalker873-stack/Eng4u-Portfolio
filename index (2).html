04 · Blog post

More Than a Girl in Wonderland

A feminist look at the women in Lewis Carroll’s Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland

When people think about Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland, they often remember the strange characters, unusual situations, and Alice’s journey through a world that does not follow ordinary rules. Looking at the story through feminist theory made me pay closer attention to how the female characters are presented. I noticed that the women in Wonderland are not simply background characters. They have personalities, make decisions, and, in some cases, hold positions of authority.

Alice is an important example because she is the main character and takes charge of her own journey. She follows her curiosity into Wonderland and continues exploring even when the world around her is confusing. She does not wait for someone else to make every decision for her. To me, this makes Alice a capable character who has control over her own experiences. Her curiosity and willingness to question things challenge the idea that girls should always be quiet, passive, or obedient.

The Queen of Hearts presents another interesting example of female power. She is the ruler of Wonderland and expects the people around her to follow her commands. Although she is often unreasonable and frightening, she still holds authority. This is important because it shows a woman in a position that was traditionally associated with men. Her character also reminds me that having power does not automatically mean using it fairly or wisely.

Using feminist theory helped me understand that representation is not only about whether a female character is good or bad. It is also about what roles she is allowed to have, how much control she has, and how other characters respond to her. Alice and the Queen are very different, but both show that women can influence what happens around them.

Overall, I think Carroll’s portrayal of female characters makes the story more interesting than it might seem at first. Through Alice’s independence and the Queen’s authority, the book gives readers examples of women who are capable of acting and holding power. A feminist perspective helped me look beyond the fantasy and recognize how the story can challenge traditional expectations of women.

The women of Wonderland may be very different from one another, but they are not powerless or unimportant to the story.
03 · Looking back

My reflection

My ENG4U work represents my growth in understanding and responding to literature. At the beginning, I often focused on what happened in a story. As I worked through the course, I became more aware that stories can be interpreted in different ways and that details such as characterization, language, and relationships can reveal deeper ideas.

Learning about literary theories helped me approach texts from more than one perspective. For example, using feminist theory when writing about Alice's Adventures in Wonderland encouraged me to think about how female characters are represented and how their actions challenge expectations. I also developed my ability to create a clear thesis, support my ideas with evidence, and explain why that evidence matters.

This collection shows more than individual assignments. It represents the skills and confidence I have built through reading, planning, drafting, and revising. I can use what I learned in ENG4U in future courses and in situations where clear communication and thoughtful interpretation are important.

A portfolio shows not just what I have completed, but how my thinking and writing have developed along the way.
